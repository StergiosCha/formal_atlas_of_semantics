# Counting, belief and events in TTR, MTT and Montague

The starting point is *Types and the Structure of Meaning* by Stergios Chatzikyriakidis, Robin Cooper, Eleni Gregoromichelaki and Peter R. Sutton (2025). Section 2 compares attitudes and §3.3 compares individuation under quantification. Here the three approaches are compared on the same examples. What makes an object count as a different book, a farmer count as a witness, or two descriptions count as the same belief? [Cambridge Element](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009285322) · [Sources, editions and locators](CMM_SOURCES.md) · [Rocq statements and examples](CMM_Comparison_spec.v)

## 1. How many books did John pick up and master?

Cooper has an analysis of this class of phenomena. In his 2011 paper a book frame connects an individual to a physical object and an informational object. Consider his shelf with two copies of *War and Peace*, two of *Ulysses*, and six of the Bible. Counting distinct values of the physical field gives ten. Counting distinct works gives three, provided editions and translations are being identified at that level. He explicitly discusses changing that criterion. His 2023 book also treats passengers counted by person or by journey. A subsequent discourse about signing a petition can favour the person reading. [Cooper 2011, §§6–7, (34)–(35)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-22221-4_5) · [Cooper 2023, §5.8, pp. 215–225, (131)–(143)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001)

Chatzikyriakidis and Luo treat a common noun together with a criterion of identity, expressed by a setoid. Their theory also uses predicates. In the book example, *pick up* takes a physical object and *master* takes an informational object. Both can apply to a book through the appropriate coercion. For the reading of *John picked up and mastered three books* in (5.48), we have to find **the same three books**, pairwise different as copies and pairwise different as works. The combined relation that rules out counting two witnesses separately is “same copy **or** same work”. This relation is reflexive and symmetric and can fail transitivity. The 2020 book therefore uses the term **pre-setoid** for the dot-type construction. Rocq's equality on a pair of identifiers would give a different count. The source numeral means *at least three* here. [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2018, §§3–4](https://doi.org/10.5617/osla.6679) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2020, §§5.3.3–5.3.4, definitions 5.5–5.6, (5.47)–(5.55)](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252) · [Authors’ Rocq formulation, copred_dd2](https://github.com/StergiosCha/formal_atlas_of_semantics/blob/f6774ec0adc45e90096eb41e4f9310cb91a4a9d5/ttr_mtt/Book_Individuation.v#L138)

The following are finite models. Every copy–work entry in this table satisfies the two lexical predicates. Each row changes only the assignments of copies to works.

| Eligible books | Distinct copies | Distinct works | Can the same three satisfy both criteria? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Three copies of one work | 3 | 1 | No |
| Three works bound in one volume | 1 | 3 | No |
| Three volumes, each with a different work | 3 | 3 | Yes |
| Volume A contains X, Y and Z. Volumes B and C each contain X | 3 | 3 | No |

In the last row, choosing books in both B and C repeats X. To obtain different works Y and Z, the choice would have to use A twice. Cooper's one-label operation computes each column separately. Even having both column totals cannot decide the last column. This crossed example is ours. It assumes an anthology can supply more than one work under the same physical identifier. Chatzikyriakidis and Luo explicitly adopt this possibility for the analysis while questioning how naturally volumes and books are identified in such cases. If copies determine a unique work in the intended model, different works already require different copies. Three copies of one work remain a test that requires no anthology assumption. [Rocq shelf and crossed cases](CMM_Comparison_spec.v#L42) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2020, p. 102 n. 4](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252)

**When do the two criteria apply together?** Cooper (2011) accounts for the single-criterion readings. Gotham (§4.2) explicitly identifies the missing rule for imposing both criteria on the same plurality. He also criticizes the *2015* MTT treatment for an invalid inference. The later MTT account addresses it. Picking up three copies and mastering everything on the shelf need not amount to mastering three distinct works. The question remains live because the choice of criterion can itself depend on the discourse. [Gotham 2017, §§4.2–4.3](https://doi.org/10.1093/jos/ffw008) · [Cambridge Element, §§3.3.2–3.3.3, especially (77)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009285322)

Sutton (2024) gives a later TTR analysis with an explicit counting base and a question under discussion. Consider *Three insightful books are damaged*. In a neutral context the analysis gives double distinctness. If librarians have already separated the insightful books for a display, *insightful* can instead tell the hearer which pile is intended. Three damaged copies of one insightful work can then suffice. A further modifier can reintroduce an informational counting requirement. Our Rocq example checks the two truth values for the same shelf once the counting base is fixed. Reproducing Sutton's derivation of the base from a question and an utterance is a separate proof obligation. This is a concrete point of comparison with the MTT account, including its provision for context-sensitive criteria. An empirical comparison should hold the sentence and shelf fixed and vary the preceding exchange. [Sutton 2024, §§2–4, examples (4)–(11)](https://doi.org/10.18148/sub/2024.v28.1167) · [Cambridge Element, §3.3.3, pp. 54–55](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009285322) · [Finite display example](CMM_Comparison_spec.v#L312)

**Where Montague enters.** PTQ has common nouns, determiner formation and quantifying-in. Its basic vocabulary omits *book* and the numeral *three*. At an index an enriched Montagovian model can provide a book predicate, relations to its physical and informational aspects, and an existential formula for three eligible witnesses whose aspects are pairwise distinct. This yields the reading of (5.48). Which noun and modifiers supply those relations, and how a question changes the counting base, are additional lexical and compositional questions. Retoré's Montagovian Generative Lexicon already gives a typed treatment of copredication using lexical transformations, illustrated by *Liverpool is vast and voted*. Compatibility of two predications and the cardinality of a qualified plurality are two distinct observations. [Montague, PTQ, §§1, 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Retoré, (9)–(11)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.4938) · [Cambridge Element, §3](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009285322)

## 2. Quantification and anaphora

For *Every man walks*, compare three domains. In a fixed TTR assignment, take the individuals for which the noun type has a situation witness. In MTT, quantify over the noun type itself and map its inhabitants to individuals. In the extensional clause of a Montague analysis, quantify over individuals satisfying the noun predicate. If these extensions agree and the verb is interpreted through the same individual, the three universal statements are equivalent. The Rocq proof uses the existing Atlas noun-image theorem. It needs neither an injective map nor proof irrelevance. Several noun witnesses may map to one individual without changing this universal truth condition. That is exactly why agreement here cannot establish agreement about *three*. PTQ’s full common-noun translation is a predicate of individual concepts, type ⟨⟨s,e⟩,t⟩. The fixed-index comparison uses the ordinary extensional reading. Postulate 2 of PTQ restricts ordinary basic common nouns to constant concepts, with explicit exceptions for *price* and *temperature*. [Cooper 2023, §1.4.1 (10) and §7.2](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [PTQ §§2–3, T2 and postulate 2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Three-way proof](CMM_Comparison_spec_probe.v#L18)

### The five farmers

*Most farmers who own a donkey like it* gives a stronger comparison. Cooper explicitly cites Luo (2021) at the point where he explains why the denominator matters. Four farmers each own one donkey and dislike it. A fifth owns fifty and likes them all. **50 of 54 ownership pairs satisfy the predicate, but just 1 of 5 farmers does.** The example runs Cooper's population through the existing Rocq interpretations of Luo's pair, strong and weak readings. Cooper cites the shorter workshop paper. The Atlas implementation follows Luo’s longer author manuscript. The pair majority holds. Both farmer majorities fail. Those two farmer readings coincide on this population because each farmer likes all or none of their own donkeys. A second instance, where each farmer likes one of two, separates them. Cooper uses local accommodation and a farmer-based restrictor. Luo distinguishes strong sums of dependent data from a weak existential in the way he forms the collection of owners. The comparison preserves the truth of specified readings while keeping these analyses of anaphora distinct. [Cooper 2023, §8.3, (64)–(66) and p. 369](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Luo implementation](https://github.com/StergiosCha/formal_atlas_of_semantics/blob/f6774ec0adc45e90096eb41e4f9310cb91a4a9d5/atlas/mtt_ranta/Donkey_Sums.v) · [Cooper's five-farmer instance](CMM_Comparison_spec.v#L68)

Montague gives an analysis tree for *Every man loves a woman such that she loves him*. Indexed pronouns and quantifying-in determine binding and scope. Cooper compares this device directly with the storage technique he adapts from his 1983 work in §8.2. PTQ's basic determiners are *every*, *a/an* and *the*. A later generalized-quantifier treatment adds *most*. Even once the numerical test is fixed, an analysis still has to say what the restrictor contains and how the pronoun accesses the donkey. Cooper's witness sets also carry conditions of their own. For example he takes an existential witness set to be a singleton, while explicitly distinguishing his choice from Barwise and Cooper (1981). A TTR witness set for a quantifier, a donkey accessible in discourse and an inhabitant of an MTT noun type each answer a different question. [PTQ §§1, 3, S14/T14](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Cooper 2023, §§7.2.4, 8.2–8.3](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001)

## 3. Belief, intention and modality

**Belief.** Montague includes *believe that*. A complement denotes a proposition whose extension at each world–time pair is a truth value. Equal intensions provide the same argument to the attitude verb. One subtlety, made explicitly in the joint Element, matters here. Treating belief as a relation to these propositions does **not** force an agent who believes P to believe every Q entailed by P. A relation can distinguish a proposition from a strictly weaker one. It cannot distinguish two presentations of the very same proposition if that proposition is its only content argument. Both facts have separate finite checks. [PTQ §§1–4, postulate 6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Cambridge Element, §2, pp. 18–20](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009285322)

In Cooper's Paderewski example, two independently acquired descriptions can concern one man. In the belief semantics, a content record type can match a type in the agent’s long-term memory up to relabelling. The initial biconditional in (40) is revised in (58) to give sufficient conditions including a point of view. A second diagnostic is Cooper's *happy that* example. Chris can welcome Kim's purchase without welcoming Sam's sale, although the same transaction occurred. Cooper specifically uses *happy* for this contrast and comments on why ordinary *believe* is a poorer choice for the buy/sell pair. Our code represents the two descriptions with the same truth at every toy index and opposite values for the reported attitude. No operation on those truth intensions alone can recover the two judgements. It checks which information was lost, without purporting to decide how a human matches descriptions. [Cooper 2023, §4.5 and §6.5, (35)–(40), (58)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Report example](CMM_Comparison_spec.v#L138)

Chatzikyriakidis and Luo have a direct analysis of **belief**, in addition to their treatment of intensional modifiers. In *Using Signatures in Type Theory to Represent Situations* they extend Ranta's belief contexts with local subtyping and discuss manifest entries. Consider John believing that all woodchucks are woodchucks. The corresponding report about groundhogs depends on what the agent’s context licenses. Their 2020 book revisits closure under derivability for *alleged*, *allegedly* and *intentionally*. Formula (4.79) requires that the action took place and belongs to the agent’s intentions. Formula (4.80) requires an allegation, with no corresponding actuality conjunct. The Oedipus example tests where the identity of Jocaste and Oedipus’s mother is available. If both descriptions have already become the same argument in the narrator's formalization, applying a collection to that argument cannot separate them. [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2015, §4, example (5) and definition 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-48119-6_13) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2017, pp. 74–75](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10849-017-9246-2) · [2020, §§3.3.4, 4.5.3–4.5.4](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252)

**Necessity.** In PTQ, □φ at one index requires φ at every world–time pair. Cooper first defines necessary inhabitation across a modal system of type assignments. There is an exact conditional result here. Associate an assignment with each PTQ index, let those assignments be the whole family under consideration, and require truth of φ at an index to agree with inhabitation of its associated type in that assignment. Rocq then proves equivalence between the actual Atlas PTQ □ clause and the Atlas TTR family-necessity predicate. This is a comparison with Cooper's §6.3. His linguistic account of *must* in §6.4 includes a base, a situation, and in the later version a *topos*, whose output depends on the situation. The broccoli example illustrates what can make something obligatory. The finite tests change which indices are relevant and obtain a different verdict. An MTT collection of an agent’s allegations has its own lexical conditions. None of the sources supplies a reason to identify it with unrestricted □. [PTQ §2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Cooper 2023, §§6.3–6.4, (13), (23)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Proof with the assumptions displayed](CMM_Comparison_spec_probe.v#L6)

## 4. Adjectives, adverbs and event types

**Adjectives.** Cooper’s 2011 *delicious lunch* example selects the food aspect of a lunch, while *took forever* concerns its event aspect. Rajestari, Dobnik, Cooper and Karimi also studied *essential, necessary, crucial, vital* and the comparison adjective *important*, with and without *very*, in English discourse contexts. This is a study of judgements using contextual replacements. A full TTR semantics for gradability cannot be read off its experiment. Chatzikyriakidis and Luo give explicit accounts of intersective, subsective, privative and non-committal adjectives and subsequently gradable and multidimensional ones. A *skilful surgeon* is a surgeon, but someone skilled at surgery may lack skill as a violinist. An *alleged murderer* need never have killed anyone. Chatzikyriakidis and Cooper themselves work through *tall girl* and *alleged* in their joint Oxford survey, using dependent types and discussing the MTT analysis. PTQ has no productive category of attributive adjectives in its printed fragment. The later Montagovian literature does analyze *former* and *alleged* as modifiers of properties. Distinguishing these examples requires lexical assumptions beyond supplying an input and output type. [Cooper 2011, §§2–4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-22221-4_5) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Cooper 2018, §§2.3–2.4 and the discussion of belief contexts](https://doi.org/10.1093/acrefore/9780199384655.013.329) · [Rajestari et al., paper](https://doi.org/10.3384/ecp184174) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10849-017-9246-2) · [Dowty, Wall and Peters, chs. 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-009-9065-4)

**On adverbs, the overlap is narrower.** Cooper expressly discusses modal sentence adverbs in §6.4. The Cooper sources examined here supply neither a manner-adverb analysis corresponding to the MTT clause for *illegibly* nor a parallel classification of manner, agent-oriented, speech-act, domain and intensional adverbs. PTQ itself lists *rapidly, slowly, voluntarily, allegedly* as IV modifiers, and *necessarily* as a sentence modifier. The shared IV-modifier category licenses composition. It does not decide whether the unmodified sentence follows. For Chatzikyriakidis and Luo, *John wrote illegibly* requires one event which is both a writing and illegible. An illegible event elsewhere cannot make John's writing illegible. *John allegedly wrote* can hold on their analysis even if there was no writing. These two cases are also distinguishable in an enriched Montagovian model. [PTQ §1 and translation rules T9–T10](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Cooper 2023, §§6.1, 6.4](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2020, §§4.5.1–4.5.4, (4.58), (4.79)–(4.80)](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252)

**Dependent event types already connect the two traditions.** Luo and Soloviev (2017) develop both a Montagovian calculus Cₑ extending Church’s simple type theory and an MTT system UTT[E]. Their §4.3, theorem 2, gives the translation from the former into the latter, covering contexts, types, typing, provable propositions and subtyping. A different theorem in Chatzikyriakidis and Luo (2020), theorem 7.2, concerns conservativity of Cₑ over Church's original system. Historical PTQ has worlds, times and individuals, with no primitive event sort. Describing either theorem as a result about PTQ's English grammar would change its subject. [Luo and Soloviev 2017, §§2–5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-55386-2_15) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2020, §7.2, pp. 157–163](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252)

*John kissed Mary passionately* supplies a three-way test. In Luo and Soloviev’s (3), one event in Evt_AP(John,Mary) witnesses both *kiss* and *passionate*. Its agent and patient can be forgotten in either order while referring to the same occurrence. In a Cooper-style assignment, the corresponding question is whether a particular situation witnesses the predicate type with these participants. At a Montague index the ordinary binary extension tells us whether John kissed Mary. We constructed two finite assignments with exactly the same pairs of kissing individuals. Each has a kiss involving John and Mary. Only the first has a passionate kiss. Rocq checks agreement for **every** participant pair in both assignments between the role-indexed existential, the TTR witness test and our binary relation in the Atlas IL semantic domain. The lexical relation *kiss* is supplied for this experiment. A projected binary relation suffices to answer the bare question and loses the distinction required by the adverb. A Montagovian event analysis can retain the occurrence and answer both. [Luo and Soloviev 2017, (1)–(4)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-55386-2_15) · [Cooper 2023, §1.4, hug and touch](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Rocq module Kiss](CMM_Comparison_spec.v#L241)

*No dog barks* poses a second question. An event binder over Evt_A(x) requires an agent x already in scope. Moving the binder outside the quantifier introducing x leaves x free in what should be a closed sentence. The 2017 paper explicitly shows this in each system. Our small scope test checks that dependency and the corresponding unrestricted event binder. A TTR record field whose type depends on a participant also needs access to that participant. An event-scope comparison between complete grammars would require a translation of binders, internal references and admissible readings. Sharing the role values in the kiss example establishes only the lexical result above. [Luo and Soloviev 2017, §5, (8)–(12)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-55386-2_15)

## 5. Other overlaps and the question about foundations

| Phenomenon | What can be compared with Montague |
|---|---|
| Proper names, identity, and points of view | PTQ postulate 1 fixes the denotations of its basic proper names across indices. Cooper distinguishes learning two descriptions from learning that they identify one individual. In the signatures approach the location of an identity or subtyping assumption matters for an attitude report. The woodchuck and Paderewski examples give concrete tests of what the reporter and the agent can each use. |
| Scope and opaque transitive verbs | PTQ derives specific and nonspecific readings of *John seeks a unicorn*. Cooper analyzes *worship* relative to a religious belief and a point of view, and separately adapts quantifier storage. These are related opacity problems with different lexical entries. A counterpart deriving the PTQ *seek* readings was not identified in the MTT sources inspected. |
| Descriptions and change | PTQ blocks the inference from *The temperature is ninety* and *The temperature rises* to *Ninety rises*. Cooper's temperature frames and scale also distinguish the changing temperature from the number. This is a documented agreement on a judgement, with different semantic objects. The two Chatzikyriakidis and Luo studies of individuation and modification examined here supply no independent MTT analysis of this example. |
| Coordination and lexical coercions | PTQ provides coordination rules at specified categories. Chatzikyriakidis and Luo use coercive subtyping and a common predicate domain in *pick up and master*. Cooper's lunch supports food and event predication within the same frame. Retoré also shows why some combinations of lexical shifts can be blocked. A truth-functional conjunction becomes applicable only after both conjuncts have received interpretations. |
| Vagueness, grades and context | Cooper and coauthors investigate contextual gradability. C&L distinguish gradability and multidimensionality and identify vagueness as an open issue, citing probabilistic TTR research. PTQ's bivalent interpretation can compare extensions across indices once they are supplied. An account of how speakers choose thresholds or disagree needs further structure. |
| Situations and records | For Cooper, a particular situation can witness a type, and a model assigns extensions to types. For Chatzikyriakidis and Luo, a signature can describe an incomplete situation. One can ask which assertions follow from it, or under which assignments it is satisfied. A situation, a description of a situation and a total Montague index play different roles. |

[Montague, PTQ §§1–4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-010-2506-5_10) · [Cooper 2023, chs. 4–8 and appendix A9](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2020, §§1.4, 2.1, 3.2, 4.6, 5.1–5.4](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252) · [Joint Element, §§1–4](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009285322)

Cooper describes his own 2011 noun analysis as combining a Montagovian function with a type-theoretic result. Applying the function returns a record type, whose witnesses can be situations. In 2023 his assignments make precise what it means for a type to have an extension in a model. Chatzikyriakidis and Luo explain their claim that MTT-semantics is both proof-theoretic and model-theoretic in a different way. Inference and typing have rules. The rich type theory also serves as the language in which individuals, collections and situations can be represented and semantic interpretations given. For an ordered pair of dependent fields, a Martin-Löf-style Σ-type already packages a first component and a second component of the corresponding type. Adding labels to this example supplies no linguistic separation. Chatzikyriakidis and Luo themselves credit Tasistro and Betarte as influences on early TTR (§1.4, p. 17 n. 28). Both traditions admit dependent structure. The studies above locate agreement at the level of truth, and test what else survives when counting criteria, evidence about an event, or the presentation of a belief matters. [Cooper 2011, §7](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-22221-4_5) · [Cooper 2023, §1.4 and appendix A9](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192871312.001.0001) · [Chatzikyriakidis and Luo 2020, §§1.4.2–1.4.3](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119489252)

## What Rocq checked

Rocq 8.20.1 checked 25 examples and two general results. The general proofs cover ordinary universal quantification at a fixed interpretation and unrestricted necessity over the family of assignments specified in the theorem. Each proof states its hypotheses. All 27 new proofs passed the independent kernel recheck and report “Closed under the global context”. The assumptions log separately lists the lexical and ontology parameters of the earlier book development. The proposed general results on three-witness preservation, forgetting event roles in either order, and unique work per copy have statements only. Independent review of the source interpretations remains open.
