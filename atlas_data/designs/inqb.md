Target: `/Users/graogro/Dropbox/revisiting-formal-semantics/atlas/inquisitive/InqB.v` (single file; `mkdir -p atlas/inquisitive` first). Coq 8.20.1, stdlib only. Build from the repo root:

## Judgment

Both designs were spot-checked against the PDFs (CR11 Def. 2.3, Props. 2.4–2.6, 2.10, 2.12, Defs. 2.13–2.15, Props. 2.18–2.25, Def. 3.1, Props. 3.2–3.10, Remark 3.8, Thm. 3.34, Cor. 3.35, Thm. 5.5, App. A.1/A.3/A.5; R13 Def. 5–7, 9, 12, Facts 2–8, 11–15, Thms. 1–3; CGR18 page scans for Facts 2.18–2.19 (p. 25), 3.3–3.5 (p. 51), Def. 3.13 + Facts 3.14–3.15 (p. 55), Def. 4.3 + Fact 4.4 (p. 61), Defs. 4.5–4.7 + Fact 4.8 (p. 62), Def. 4.9–4.12 (p. 64), Fact 4.13 (p. 65), Facts 4.14–4.15 (p. 70), 4.16–4.17 (p. 71), 4.18–4.19 (p. 72); C16 Defs. 2.1.1, 2.1.3, 2.2.2, 2.4.1, Props. 2.2.3, 2.2.7–2.2.9, 2.4.3, 2.5.5–2.5.6, Cor. 2.5.11, Props. 2.5.16–2.5.17, 2.5.21). Both are faithful; both reconstruct the book's numbering correctly (A's sequential count and B's page-scan numbers agree: Fact 3.14 = Division, Fact 4.16 = support/truth). Corrections: A mislabels the book's chapters (ch. 3 is "Basic operations on propositions", ch. 4 "A first-order inquisitive semantics" — the labels in the brief are informal); A's claim that the singleton collapse needs `classic` is false once the valuation is Boolean (B's probe: the `→` case closes with a `bool` case split, persistence and the empty-state lemma); B under-flags that the *abstract* non-informativity of `?P` (R13 Thm. 3 "⋃[?φ] = W") needs excluded middle on `info P`, exactly like `!P = P**`; B's artifact (i) "none expected" is wrong in spirit — the strict-positivity constraint is precisely *why* support must be a `Fixpoint`, and that is worth recording.

**Decision: Design B (semantics-first, Boolean valuation, constructive core) is the base.** Reasons: (1) with `V : W -> Atom -> bool` every MUST theorem of the brief is provable with no axioms at all (probe-verified, `Closed under the global context`), which is a stronger deliverable than A's `Classical` import; the choice is faithful (C16 Def. 2.1.1 has `V : W × P → {0,1}`; CR11's worlds are subsets of `P`, i.e. `Atom -> bool`). (2) B's explicit-comprehension-state discipline (no "take the maximal state" arguments) is what makes the proofs go through without finiteness. (3) B's family meets/joins over `Σ : iprop -> Prop` are R13's "any set of propositions" literally.

**Grafted from A** (all cheap, all MUST unless noted): the second interpretation `alg : form -> iprop` transcribing CGR18 Def. 4.3 / R13 Def. 7 verbatim, with the coincidence theorem `alg f = sem f` (R13 Fact 7) as a genuine metatheorem; the split of the Hilbert system into `Inductive axiom` (twelve schemes) + `Inductive deriv` (three constructors), so soundness is one lemma per scheme; the truth-conditional-antecedent lemma (C16 Prop. 2.2.9) as the KP workhorse instead of an ad-hoc comprehension state; the exact characterisation `⊨ ¬¬φ→φ ⟺ φ truth-conditional` (C16 Prop. 2.5.17), the LEM characterisation, uniform-substitution failure via a `subst` function (CR11 Remark 3.8, C16 Prop. 2.5.21); "only ⊤, ⊥ have Boolean complements" (CGR18 p. 51–52 / R13 p. 88); "two incomparable alternatives ⇒ inquisitive"; CR11 Props. 3.2–3.4; `Print Assumptions` lines as documentation; A's pitfalls on `bigjoin`'s `{∅}` convention, the KP unfolding maze, the M2 countermodel state, and `sub_refl/sub_trans` hints. **Dropped**: A's `Classical` import in the core (moved to an optional fenced epilogue), A's bundled `iprop` record as the primary carrier (a raw predicate plus `is_prop` is lighter; the bundle appears only as `proposition := {P | is_prop P}` for the literal "⟦φ⟧ ∈ 𝒫" statement), A's unicode notations (ASCII only), B's schemata-as-`deriv`-constructors.

Source abbreviations and pagination: **CGR18** = Ciardelli, Groenendijk & Roelofsen 2018 (printed page; PDF page = printed + 13; `pdftotext` drops all numerals, use the page scans); **R13** = Roelofsen 2013, Synthese 190 (journal page); **CR11** = Ciardelli & Roelofsen 2011 (author-manuscript page); **C16** = Ciardelli 2016 dissertation (printed page; PDF page = printed + 18).

## 1. Scope

### Formalized — MUST (propositional InqB over an arbitrary logical space)

1. Information states over an arbitrary `W : Type`; enhancement; ignorant / inconsistent / singleton states — CGR18 Def. 2.1–2.2 (p. 16), p. 17; C16 Def. 2.1.1 (p. 46).
2. Propositions (= issues) as non-empty, downward-closed sets of states; `info`, truth, support-as-membership, informative/inquisitive, alternatives (maximal elements), greatest element, entailment as inclusion, ⊤/⊥ — CGR18 Defs. 2.3–2.4, 2.7, 2.9–2.16, 2.20–2.24, Facts 2.14, 2.18, 2.19, 2.23, 2.25; R13 Defs. 1–5, Fact 2.
3. The algebra of propositions: binary and arbitrary meets/joins, relative pseudo-complement `⇒`, absolute pseudo-complement `*`, the Heyting law, only ⊤/⊥ have Boolean complements — CGR18 Facts 3.1–3.5 (pp. 49–51), pp. 51–52; R13 Def. 6, Facts 3–6 (pp. 86–88).
4. Projections `!`, `?`; decision sets; representation/uniqueness theorems; division `P = !P ∩ ?P`; `!P = P**` (constructively `!P ⊆ P**`, equality under EM on `info P`, discharged for all sentences) — CGR18 Defs. 3.6–3.13, Facts 3.7, 3.10, 3.12, 3.14, 3.15 (pp. 53–55); R13 Thms. 2–3, Facts 11, 14 (pp. 95–97).
5. Syntax `p | ⊥ | ∧ | ∨ | →`, abbreviations `¬φ := φ→⊥`, `!φ := ¬¬φ`, `?φ := φ∨¬φ`; support semantics; `⟦φ⟧`, `info(φ)`, `alt(φ)`, truth at `w` as support at `{w}`; classical truth `|φ|`; the algebraic recursion of CGR18 Def. 4.3 and its coincidence with support — CR11 §2 (p. 3), Def. 2.3; CGR18 Defs. 4.2–4.3, 4.5–4.7, Fact 4.8(1–5), Def. 4.11, Fact 4.12; R13 Defs. 7, 9, Facts 7, 12; C16 Defs. 2.1.3, 2.2.2, 2.2.5.
6. Persistence, empty-state property, `⟦φ⟧ ∈ 𝒫` — CR11 Prop. 2.4; CGR18 Fact 4.4; C16 Prop. 2.2.3.
7. Truth-conditional collapse `{w} ⊨ φ ⟺ w ⊨_cl φ` for all φ; negation is pointwise; `info(φ) = |φ|` — CR11 Props. 2.5, 2.6, 2.21; CGR18 Fact 4.14; R13 Fact 8; C16 Props. 2.1.7, 2.2.4.
8. Sentence-level categories and their characterisations: non-inquisitive ⟺ truth-conditional ⟺ `φ ≡ !φ` ⟺ `[φ] = ℘(|φ|)`; non-informative ⟺ classical tautology ⟺ `φ ≡ ?φ`; `!φ` non-inquisitive with `info(!φ) = info(φ)`; `?φ` non-informative; division `φ ≡ !φ ∧ ?φ`; idempotence; sufficient syntactic conditions; `[¬φ] = {|¬φ|}` — CGR18 Defs. 4.9, Facts 4.10, 4.13–4.18; CR11 Props. 2.12, 2.18, 2.19, 2.23–2.25; R13 Facts 9, 13.
9. The classical (∨-free) fragment is truth-conditional; entailment into a non-inquisitive conclusion is classical; `!φ` is the strongest assertion entailed by φ; questions entail only questions — CR11 Cor. 2.20, Props. 3.2–3.4; CGR18 Fact 4.19; R13 Fact 10; C16 Prop. 2.1.8, 2.3.6–2.3.9.
10. Entailment as inclusion, equivalence, validity; validity ⟺ support at the ignorant state; per-model disjunction property; deduction theorem; `InqL ⊆ CPL`; validity ⟺ tautology ∧ non-inquisitive — CGR18 Def. 4.6, Fact 2.23; CR11 Def. 3.1, 3.5, Props. 3.6, 3.7, 3.9, 3.10, §3.3; C16 Prop. 2.5.16.
11. `⟦φ∧ψ⟧ = ⟦φ⟧ ∩ ⟦ψ⟧`, `⟦φ∨ψ⟧ = ⟦φ⟧ ∪ ⟦ψ⟧`, `⟦φ→ψ⟧ = ⟦φ⟧ ⇒ ⟦ψ⟧` with the adjunction `ρ ⊆ ⟦φ→ψ⟧ ⟺ ρ ∩ ⟦φ⟧ ⊆ ⟦ψ⟧` for propositions ρ; hence a Heyting algebra — CGR18 Def. 4.3, Fact 3.3; R13 Def. 7, Fact 5, p. 88.
12. Failure of DNE and LEM: `⊨ ¬¬φ→φ ⟺ φ truth-conditional`; concrete countermodels (`p∨¬p`, `¬¬?p→?p` in a 2-world model; `¬¬(p∨q)→(p∨q)`, hybridity and the two alternatives of `p∨q` in the 4-world model); atomic DNE holds; no closure under uniform substitution — CR11 §3.3 p. 14, Remark 3.8, Exs. 2.11, 2.16, 2.17; CGR18 §4.4, Fig. 4.1; C16 Props. 2.5.17, 2.5.21.
13. Soundness of `KPⁿ` = IPL (9 schemes) + KP + atomic `¬¬p→p`, MP only — CR11 App. A.1, A.3, A.5 (pp. 31–32), Cor. 3.35 (p. 18), Thm. 5.5 soundness half (p. 22); R13 Thm. 1 (p. 91). Twelve schemes: short, hence MUST.

### STRETCH
Cross-model disjunction property and Split property via the disjoint-union model (C16 Props. 2.5.5–2.5.6); resolutions and inquisitive normal form (C16 Def. 2.4.1, Prop. 2.4.3); normality / maximal supporting states (CR11 Prop. 2.10; R13 Fact 15; C16 Prop. 2.2.7) and the finite-case converse of CGR18 Fact 2.17 under an explicit maximality hypothesis; Kripke correspondence (CR11 Def. 3.18, Prop. 3.19); a `bool` decision procedure for listed finite `W` (CR11 Remark 3.12); Leibniz-equality corollaries under `FunctionalExtensionality` + `PropExtensionality` in a fenced epilogue; `?!φ ≠ !?φ` countermodel; completeness (route documented only).

### Deliberately out
First-order InqB (CGR18 Def. 4.3(6–7), R13 §3.4, C16 ch. 4); contexts and update (CGR18 §2.5); compliance/maximal possibilities (R13 §3.9); CR11 §§3.2–7 beyond soundness (compactness, dnt, negative variants, Medvedev logic, schematic fragment, inquisitive hierarchy); natural-language chapters (CGR18 ch. 5–9). Facts that genuinely need finiteness (CGR18 Facts 2.8, 2.17 "⇒", CR11 Prop. 2.10) appear only in the general direction or under an explicit hypothesis; the sources flag them themselves (CGR18 fn. 6 p. 25, R13 Fact 15 "for L_P").

## 2. Representation decisions (with justification)

| Notion | Coq | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Worlds | `W : Type`, section variable; no finiteness, no decidable equality, no non-emptiness | CGR18 p. 15 / R13 p. 82 take an arbitrary set; CR11's `W = ℘(P)` is the instance `Atom -> bool`. |
| Atoms | `Atom : Type`; no countability | CR11 assumes `P` countable only for compactness (out of scope); C16 fn. 1 says it is "not strictly necessary". |
| Valuation | `V : W -> Atom -> bool` (section variable) | C16 Def. 2.1.1 `V : W×P → {0,1}` literally; CR11 Def. 2.1 (`p ∈ w`, decidable membership). Makes the singleton collapse, atomic DNE, `?φ` non-informativity and hence the whole MUST list axiom-free. A `Prop`-valued `V` would push EM into T27, T33, T60 and buy nothing (the sources are classical anyway). |
| Canonical model (CR11) | `canonW Atom := Atom -> bool`, `canonV w p := w p` | CR11 Defs. 2.1–2.2 verbatim; used to instantiate CR11 statements, not needed for any MUST proof. |
| State | `state W := W -> Prop`; `sub t s := forall w, t w -> s w`; `empty s := forall w, ~ s w`; `ignorant := fun _ => True`; `single w := fun v => v = w`; `inter`, `pow a := fun s => sub s a` | Plain predicates: `simpl`/`unfold` predictable, no `Ensembles` (its `Same_set` needs `Extensionality_Ensembles`, and `In`/`Included` clash with `List`). The `⊥`-clause uses the *predicate* `empty s`, not `s = ∅`: substates produced by the `→` clause are only extensionally empty (artifact iv). |
| Set of states / proposition | `iprop W := state W -> Prop`; `down_closed P`; `is_prop P := (exists s, P s) /\ down_closed P`; `proposition := { P | is_prop P }` (bundle, used only to state `⟦φ⟧ ∈ 𝒫` and the Heyting record) | CGR18 Def. 2.9 (p. 21) / R13 Def. 5 (p. 86). Raw predicate + side condition keeps `∩ ∪ ⇒` the literal predicate operations and avoids dependent-record rewriting. Issues (CGR18 Def. 2.3) are the same type; noted in a comment. |
| Equality of states / propositions | `seq s t := forall w, s w <-> t w`; `peq P Q := forall s, P s <-> Q s`; never Leibniz in the core | Source equations (`P = !P ∩ ?P`, `[φ→ψ] = [φ] ⇒ [ψ]`, `info(φ) = |φ|`) become `peq`/`seq`. Downward closure makes every proposition respect `seq` automatically, so no `Proper`/setoid machinery is needed (only `Require Import Setoid` for `rewrite` with `<->`). Leibniz versions are corollaries in the fenced epilogue under stdlib extensionality axioms (artifact iv). Exception: the five clause equations `sem (FAnd f g) = pmeet (sem f) (sem g)` etc. *are* Leibniz, by `reflexivity`, because `support` is defined so that its clauses are the operators. |
| Families | `Σ : iprop W -> Prop`; `pMeet Σ s := forall P, Σ P -> P s`; `pJoin Σ s := empty s \/ exists P, Σ P /\ P s` | R13 Facts 3–4 "any set of propositions Σ"; the empty family gives `℘(W)` automatically for the meet, and the `empty s` disjunct realises the `{∅}` stipulation for the join (without it `pJoin ∅` is not a proposition). |
| Syntax | `Inductive form (Atom : Type) := FAtom (p : Atom) \| FBot \| FAnd \| FOr \| FImp`; abbreviations `Notation Neg f := (FImp f FBot)`, `Notation Bang f := (FImp (FImp f FBot) FBot)`, `Notation Quest f := (FOr f (FImp f FBot))` | CR11 p. 3; R13 Fact 12 (p. 97); CGR18 p. 65 "! and ? do not have to be added … as primitive connectives". `form` depends only on `Atom`, so countermodel formulas carry no spurious arguments. Notations (not `Definition`s) so that clause lemmas and `rewrite` apply to `Neg`/`Bang`/`Quest` without `unfold`. |
| Support | `Fixpoint support (s : state W) (f : form Atom) : Prop` — atom `forall w, s w -> V w p = true`; `⊥` `empty s`; `∧` `/\`; `∨` `\/`; `→` `forall t, sub t s -> support t f -> support t g` | CR11 Def. 2.3 (p. 3); CGR18 Fact 4.8(1–5) (pp. 62–63); R13 Def. 9 (p. 90); C16 Defs. 2.1.3 + 2.2.2. Must be a `Fixpoint` on the formula: the `→` clause has `support t f` in negative position, so an `Inductive` relation is rejected by strict positivity (artifact i). |
| `⟦φ⟧` | `sem f : iprop W := fun s => support s f` (all supporting states) | CGR18 Def. 4.7 (p. 62), R13 Fact 7 (p. 90). CR11 Def. 2.9's `[φ]` = *maximal* supporting states is `alt_f f`, never `sem` (R13 §3.9 fn. 5 explains the two conventions). |
| Algebraic recursion | `Fixpoint alg (f : form Atom) : iprop W` — `FAtom p ↦ pow (truthset (FAtom p))`, `FBot ↦ pbot`, `FAnd ↦ pmeet`, `FOr ↦ pjoin`, `FImp ↦ pimp` | CGR18 Def. 4.3(1–5) (p. 61), R13 Def. 7 (p. 88) transcribed verbatim (the `⊥` clause is the one CGR/R13 leave implicit, since they have `¬` primitive; `[¬φ] = [φ]*` becomes a theorem). Bridged by `alg_eq_sem`, provable as Leibniz equality by induction + `rewrite IH` + `reflexivity` (all clauses are convertible), no extensionality. |
| Classical truth | `Fixpoint ctruth (w : W) (f : form Atom) : bool`; `truthset f := fun w => ctruth w f = true` (`|φ|`); `true_at w f := support (single w) f` | CGR18 Def. 4.2 (p. 60); CR11 Def. 2.9(3); C16 Prop. 2.1.7 clauses; "truth at `w` as support at `{w}`" (C16 p. 48, CGR18 Def. 4.7). The two are bridged by the collapse theorem T27. |
| Truth-conditional | `truth_conditional f := forall s, support s f <-> forall w, s w -> ctruth w f = true` (model-relative) | CGR18 Fact 4.16 RHS (p. 71); C16 Def. 1.3.6 (there quantified over all models — ours is per model, as CGR18 Fact 4.10; noted). |
| Entailment / validity | `entails (Θ : form Atom -> Prop) f`; `entails1 f g`; `valid_in f := forall s, support s f`; `equiv f g := peq (sem f) (sem g)`; after the section: `Record model Atom := { Wm : Type; Vm : Wm -> Atom -> bool }`, `valid f := forall M s, support (Vm M) s f` | CGR18 Def. 4.6 (p. 62); CR11 Def. 3.1, 3.5 (pp. 9–10); C16 §2.5. Contexts as predicates (no `List.In` bookkeeping). |
| Hilbert system | `Inductive axiom : form Atom -> Prop` with constructors IPL1–IPL9, `ax_KP`, `ax_DNatom (p : Atom)`; `Inductive deriv (Θ) : form Atom -> Prop := d_hyp \| d_ax (axiom f) \| d_mp` | CR11 App. A.1, A.3, A.5 (pp. 31–32) verbatim; R13 Thm. 1. Atomic DNE quantifies over `p : Atom` only — otherwise soundness is false (Remark 3.8). |
| Classical / extensional reasoning | None in the core. `From Coq Require Import Classical FunctionalExtensionality PropExtensionality` only in the final fenced `Section Epilogue` | The MUST list is constructive with Boolean `V` (probe). The only residual EM is on `info P` for *abstract* `P` in `!P = P**` and "`?P` non-informative" (R13 Thms. 2–3 proofs are classical there); stated with the hypothesis `decided_info P := forall w, info P w \/ ~ info P w` and discharged for every `sem f`. |
| Notation | ASCII only, after the sections, in `Declare Scope inq_scope`: `s |= f` (support), `f |-- g` (entails1), `P <= Q` (`ple`) if wanted; no `Utf8` | `Utf8` binds `¬ ∧ ∨ → ∀`; `!`/`?` are risky tokens. Every statement must also read without notations. |

## 3. Definitions (numbered, with source refs)

Conventions: `s t u : state W`; `P Q R : iprop W`; `f g h : form Atom`; `π : iprop W -> iprop W`.

### Part 1 — syntax (`Section Syntax`, `Variable Atom : Type`)
- D1. `form Atom ::= FAtom p | FBot | FAnd f g | FOr f g | FImp f g` — CR11 §2 p. 3 (`L_P`); C16 Def. 2.1.2 + §2.2 (inquisitive disjunction is the primitive `∨`); R13 §3.3.
- D2. `Notation Neg f`, `Bang f`, `Quest f` (abbreviations, see §2) — CR11 p. 3; CGR18 Fact 4.12 (p. 64); R13 Fact 12 (p. 97); C16 Def. 2.2.5.
- D3. `orfree : form Atom -> Prop` (inductive: atoms, `FBot`, closed under `FAnd`, `FImp`) — C16 §2.1 p. 47 (`L_P^c`); CR11 Cor. 2.20 "disjunction-free".
- D4. `subst (p : Atom) (g f : form Atom) : form Atom` (uniform substitution of `g` for `p`; needs `Atom` with decidable equality only in the countermodel module, so state it for `Atom := bool`) — CR11 Remark 3.8 (p. 10); C16 Prop. 2.5.21 (p. 67).

### Part 2 — states and abstract propositions (`Section Propositions`, `Variable W : Type`)
- D5. `state`, `sub`, `empty`, `nil_state := fun _ => False`, `ignorant`, `single`, `inter`, `seq` — CGR18 Def. 2.1–2.2 (p. 16), p. 17; R13 Def. 1 (p. 84); C16 Def. 2.1.1 (p. 46).
- D6. `pow a := fun s => sub s a` (`℘(a)`) — CGR18 p. 18.
- D7. `iprop`, `down_closed`, `is_prop`, `peq`, `ple P Q := forall s, P s -> Q s`, `proposition := { P | is_prop P }` — CGR18 Def. 2.3 (p. 17), Def. 2.9 (p. 21), Def. 2.22 (p. 27); R13 Def. 5, Fact 2 (p. 86).
- D8. `resolves s P := P s`; `supports s P := P s`; `refines P Q := ple P Q` — CGR18 Defs. 2.4, 2.6, 2.13 (pp. 17–22).
- D9. `info P : state W := fun w => exists s, P s /\ s w` (`⋃P`) — CGR18 Def. 2.10 (p. 22); R13 Def. 2 (p. 84).
- D10. `ptrue P w := info P w` — CGR18 Def. 2.12 (p. 22).
- D11. `informative P := ~ forall w, info P w`; `inquisitive P := ~ P (info P)`; `noninformative`, `noninquisitive` (positive forms `forall w, info P w`, `P (info P)`); `hybrid`; `tautology_p P := P ignorant` — CGR18 Def. 2.15 (p. 23), Fact 2.18 (p. 25); R13 Defs. 10–11 (p. 92).
- D12. `alt P s := P s /\ forall t, P t -> sub s t -> sub t s` (maximal elements); `greatest P s := P s /\ forall t, P t -> sub t s` — CGR18 Def. 2.7 (p. 19), Def. 2.16 (p. 23), fn. 6 (p. 25); CR11 Def. 2.9(1).
- D13. `decided_info P := forall w, info P w \/ ~ info P w` — encoding hypothesis (see §2, artifact v).
- D14. `ptop := pow ignorant`; `pbot := fun s => empty s` (`{∅}`) — CGR18 Def. 2.24 (p. 27); R13 p. 87.
- D15. `pmeet P Q s := P s /\ Q s`; `pjoin P Q s := P s \/ Q s`; `pMeet Σ`, `pJoin Σ` (§2) — CGR18 Facts 3.1–3.2 (p. 49); R13 Facts 3–4 (pp. 86–87).
- D16. `pimp P Q s := forall t, sub t s -> P t -> Q t` (`P ⇒ Q`); `pstar P := pimp P pbot` (`P*`) — CGR18 Facts 3.3–3.4 (p. 51); R13 Def. 6 (p. 87), p. 88.
- D17. `is_glb`, `is_lub`, `is_rpc P Q R := ple (pmeet P R) Q /\ forall C, is_prop C -> ple (pmeet P C) Q -> ple C R`; `boolean_complement P Q := peq (pmeet P Q) pbot /\ peq (pjoin P Q) ptop` — CGR18 pp. 47–51; R13 pp. 83, 87–88.
- D18. `contradicts s P := forall w, s w -> ~ info P w`; `decides s P := P s \/ contradicts s P`; `dset P := fun s => decides s P` (`D(P)`) — CGR18 Defs. 3.8–3.9 (pp. 53–54); R13 Def. 12 (p. 96).

### Part 3 — semantics (`Section Semantics`, `Context {W Atom : Type} (V : W -> Atom -> bool)`)
- D21. `support` (Fixpoint, §2) — CR11 Def. 2.3 (p. 3); CGR18 Fact 4.8(1–5) (pp. 62–63); R13 Def. 9 (p. 90); C16 Defs. 2.1.3 (p. 47), 2.2.2 (p. 50). Followed by clause lemmas `support_atom … support_imp`, `support_neg_unfold`, then `Arguments support : simpl never`.
- D22. `sem f := fun s => support s f` (`⟦φ⟧`); `sem_prop f : proposition` (from T23) — CGR18 Def. 4.7 (p. 62); R13 Fact 7 (p. 90).
- D23. `ctruth`, `truthset`, `true_at` — CGR18 Def. 4.2 (p. 60); CR11 Def. 2.9(3) (p. 4); C16 Prop. 2.1.7 (p. 49), p. 48.

## 5. Expected encoding artifacts (and how to document them)

Each gets an `(* ARTIFACT (class): … *)` comment at the affected definition, a summary block in the file header, and — for the headline theorems — the `Print Assumptions` output pasted into the closing comment.

- **(i) Strict positivity.** `support` cannot be an `Inductive` relation: the `→` clause has `support t f` in negative position. It is a `Fixpoint` on `form` into `Prop` (same for `ctruth`, `alg`); consequently every metatheorem is by induction on syntax, never on a derivation of support — which matches the sources, whose proofs are all "by induction on the complexity of φ". Include a commented-out `Fail Inductive support_rel …` so the constraint is visible. `axiom`/`deriv` are ordinary positive inductives.
- **(ii) Universe / type-value collapse.** Issues, propositions, decision sets and `℘(s)` are all `iprop W`; "𝒫, the set of all propositions" is the side predicate `is_prop` (the sigma `proposition` only for packaging). Membership `s ∈ [φ]` and support `s ⊨ φ` are *literally the same `Prop`* (`sem f s ≡ support s f`), which is why R13 Fact 7 is provable by induction with `reflexivity` in every case — flag as fidelity gained by collapse. Truth at `w` is both `ctruth w f = true` (computable `bool`) and `support (single w) f` (`Prop`); T27 is the bridge. `model` must live in `Type` (`Record model … : Type`) for `valid` to quantify over it; family meets/joins over `Σ : iprop W -> Prop` need no universe care (`Prop` is impredicative).
- **(iii) Computational opacity.** `support` quantifies over all substates and `info`/`pJoin` use `exists`; nothing evaluates, even for finite `W`. Countermodels (Block G) are proved by exhibiting substates and worlds, not by `vm_compute` (T67 is the optional reflection layer). `alt` is a predicate; "the set of maximal elements" is never constructed ("the alternatives are exactly `|p|`, `|q|`" = two `alt` facts + an incomparability). `empty s` replaces `s = ∅` in the `⊥` clause and in `pbot`.
- **(iv) Missing native structure.** No Heyting-algebra/lattice/complete-lattice classes in the stdlib (and no MathComp): `is_glb`, `is_lub`, `is_rpc`, `greatest`, `alt` are hand-rolled; "⟨𝒫,⊆⟩ is a (complete) Heyting algebra" is the conjunction T6 + T9 + T10 + T11 packaged in a bespoke `Record HeytingLaws` over the explicit equivalence `peq`. Equality of propositions is extensional (`peq`/`seq`), so R13's "every two non-identical propositions really differ" (antisymmetry, p. 86) holds up to `peq`; the Leibniz versions live only in the fenced epilogue (T63). No finiteness library: CGR18 Facts 2.8/2.17 "⇒", CR11 Prop. 2.10 use an explicit hypothesis (T65). `Ensembles` deliberately not used. Contexts are predicates, not sets of formulas.
- **(v) Decidability gap — reversed.** The sources are classical set theory; with a Boolean valuation the whole MUST list is constructive (probe-verified for T10, T22–T23, T27–T28, T35, T34, T54–T55, T60–T62). The gap surfaces only for *abstract* propositions in T17 (`?P` non-informative) and T20 (`!P = P**`), where R13's proofs use "⋃[?φ] = W" / double negation; stated with `decided_info P` and discharged for every `sem f` by `decided_info_sem`. The Booleanness of `V` is a genuine (faithful) decidability assumption: a `Prop`-valued `V` would push EM into T27, T34, T61. No decidable equality on `W`, no finiteness (except T65/T67), no countability of `Atom`. Closing comment: `Print Assumptions` for `sem_is_prop`, `singleton_collapse`, `info_eq_truthset`, `imp_adjunction`, `alg_eq_sem`, `sem_imp_is_rpc`, `division_p`, `division_f`, `orfree_tc`, `dne_valid_iff_tc`, `LEM_fails_2`, `DNE_fails_or_4`, `soundness` — all expected `Closed under the global context`.

## 6. Pitfalls

1. **Generalise the state before induction.** `persistence`, `empty_state`, `singleton_collapse`, T29, T66 need IHs of the form `forall s, …`: `induction f as [p| |f1 IH1 f2 IH2|…]; intros s …` (or `revert s`). Use explicit case names and bullets; `induction f; simpl; intros; auto` silently closes some cases and misaligns the bullets that follow.
2. **Folded vs unfolded heads.** `apply H` fails with "unable to unify `empty t` with `t w -> False`" (and `sem f s` vs `support s f`, `pimp …` vs its `forall`). Keep `sub`, `empty`, `single`, `pow`, `pmeet`, `pjoin`, `pimp`, `pstar` transparent, add `Hint Unfold … : inq`, and use `exact`/`refine`/`unfold … in *` deliberately; never rely on `apply` guessing.
3. **`simpl` on `support`.** `simpl` over-unfolds `support s (Bang f)` into three nested `forall t, sub t _ -> …`. Prove the clause lemmas, then `Arguments support : simpl never`, and go through `neg_pointwise`/`bang_pointwise` (T28) instead of unfolding negation.
4. **Abbreviations.** `Neg`/`Bang`/`Quest` are *notations* (abbreviations with parameters), so `rewrite neg_pointwise in H` works on `H : support s (Bang f)`; if they were `Definition`s, `rewrite` would need `unfold`. Rewriting with `<->` needs `Require Import Setoid`.
5. **Singletons.** `single w := fun v => v = w`; a hypothesis `Hts : sub t (single w)` gives `t v -> v = w`, not `t w`. Emptiness of such `t` is decided *by `ctruth`*, never by case analysis on `t` (no EM): see the T27 strategy. `intros v Hv; unfold single in Hv; subst v` rather than `intros v ->`.
6. **No maximal states without finiteness.** Every source argument that "takes the possibility for ¬φ" (CR11 Props. 2.24, 3.3, KP) must be replaced by an explicit comprehension state (`inter s (fun w => ctruth w f = false)`) or by T29; forgetting this forces EM or finiteness.
7. **Extensionality traps.** Never state `P = Q` or `s = t` in the core; `info (pow a)` is not `a` syntactically (prove `seq`); antisymmetry of `ple` is up to `peq`. Do not `rewrite` with a `seq`/`peq` hypothesis under the `forall t, sub t s -> …` binder — use `prop_respects_seq`/`support_seq` (T2, T23) instead. The five Leibniz clause equations (T24) are the *only* `=` between sets and hold by `reflexivity`; `alg_eq_sem` is by `rewrite IH; reflexivity`.
8. **Family join convention.** `pJoin Σ s := empty s \/ …`; without the `empty s` disjunct `pJoin ∅` is `∅` not `{∅}`, T8 fails, and R13 Fact 4's stipulation is violated.
9. **Maximal vs greatest.** CGR18 fn. 6 (p. 25): a unique maximal element need not be greatest in the infinite case. Keep `alt` and `greatest` distinct; state Fact 2.19(3) with `greatest`.
10. **Two notions of `[φ]`.** CR11 Def. 2.9 `[φ]` = maximal supporting states (= `alt_f`); CGR18/R13 `[φ]` = all supporting states (= `sem`). CR11 Props. 2.12, 2.24(5), Def. 2.13 must be re-read accordingly (T40, T41).
11. **`⊥` vs primitive `¬`.** CGR18/R13 have `¬` primitive (`[¬φ] = [φ]*`) and no `⊥`; CR11/C16 have `⊥`. Follow CR11; `alg FBot := pbot` is the missing clause; `sem_neg = pstar` is derived.
12. **`bool` reasoning.** `Bool.andb_true_iff`, `orb_true_iff`, `negb_true_iff`, `implb` unfolding for T27; `destruct (ctruth w f) eqn:E`; `discriminate`/`congruence` close `true = false`. Define `ctruth` with `implb`/`negb` explicitly so `simpl` gives Boolean formulas, not `if`.
13. **KP validity.** Do not unfold five nested `forall t, sub t s`; go through `neg_antecedent` (T29) with `a := Neg f` — the dissertation's Cor. 2.5.11 proof. Register `sub_refl`, `sub_trans` with `Hint Resolve … : inq` so the IPL-axiom proofs stay short.
14. **Countermodel bookkeeping.** In `W2`, `ignorant ⊭ ¬p` needs the explicit witness `t := single true` with `sub (single true) ignorant`. In `W4`, the DNE failure must use `{11,10,01}`, not `ignorant` (world `00` falsifies `¬¬(p∨q)`); `destruct w as [[|] [|]]` systematically.
15. **Atomic DNE scheme.** `ax_DNatom` must quantify over `p : Atom` only; quantifying over formulas makes `axiom_valid` false (T56 is the counterexample), and the whole point of CR11 Remark 3.8 is lost.
16. **Section generalisation and implicits.** Inside `Section Semantics` use `Context {W Atom : Type} (V : W -> Atom -> bool)`; after `End`, `support` takes `V` explicitly (`support V2 s f`), which is what the countermodels want — do not make `V` implicit. Avoid `Set Implicit Arguments`; set implicits per constant with `Arguments`. `form`'s constructors get `Arguments FAtom {Atom}` etc.
17. **`model` universe.** `Record model (Atom : Type) : Type := …` (not `Set`); `valid f := forall M : model Atom, …` is a `Prop`, fine by impredicativity; no universe polymorphism.
18. **Name clashes.** Avoid `In`, `Included`, `Empty_set`, `Full_set`, `valid` is fine; prefix lemmas (`ple_refl`, `peq_refl`) since `Setoid` exports `Reflexive`-style names; no `Coq.Unicode.Utf8`; test-compile the `inq_scope` notations right after declaring them.
19. **`Print Assumptions` hygiene.** Import `Classical`/extensionality only in the epilogue *after* the `Print Assumptions` lines for the MUST theorems, so a constructive proof cannot silently pick up `classic` through a helper.
20. **8.20 specifics.** `From Coq Require Import …` (not `Stdlib`); `PropExtensionality` exports `propositional_extensionality`; `FunctionalExtensionality` gives the `extensionality` tactic; no `Program`/`Equations`.
