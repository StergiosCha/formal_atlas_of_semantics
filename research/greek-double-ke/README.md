# Greek double *ke*

Stergios Chatzikyriakidis and Giorgos Spathas, *Bisyndetic Distributive Conjunction and Additive ke under a Diachronic Perspective*, AMGL36, Thessaloniki, 24 April 2015. The title slide prints “Distirbutive”. The citation here normalizes that spelling. [Read the study in the Atlas](https://orange-beach-0c447e210.5.azurestaticapps.net/#/research/greek-double-ke)

## From the slides to the proof

| Passage | Meaning in the talk | Correspondence in the Rocq file |

| Slides 14 and 18 | “Both John and George praised Mary” requires that each praised her. A double additive entails this reading. | `double_entails_distribution` proves the forward direction for any candidate sets. The encoded `DIST` ranges over the two named individuals in this example. |

The premise requires each eligible individual distinct from the associate to give a different proposition. |

The Atlas makes worlds, contextually available candidates and the two components of an additive meaning explicit. In the code, `defined_true` requires both the presupposition and the assertion to hold at the world in question. The four finite examples include a counterexample to the unrestricted use of the converse in a restricted context, and another in which two individuals give the very same proposition. The type of equality on propositions here is Rocq equality of functions from worlds to propositions. The fragment covers the two-member distributive reading on slide 14. Generalized conjunction over arbitrary semantic types and the mereology of all subparts on slide 13–14 would require further work.

## Historical evidence

The authors propose that two additive uses of *ke* could be reanalysed as a distributive correlative, on the model of *te … te* and *te … ke* (slides 3 and 17–21). The proofs establish how the readings relate given a context. Establishing when speakers conventionalized one of those readings requires textual evidence.

- In *Iliad* 18.419–420, the golden attendants have intelligence, then “voice and strength” (ἐν δὲ καὶ αὐδὴ καὶ σθένος). Intelligence makes it possible to add the pair to what is already said. A reading with two locally marked nouns is also possible. [Greek text](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0012.tlg001.perseus-grc2:18.419-18.420/)
- In *Iliad* 15.497–499, καὶ οἶκος καὶ κλῆρος can be heard as “both house and holding”. The first καί may also connect the predication about the holding and the house to the preceding one about the fighter’s family. This token leaves the scope of the first καί open. [Greek text](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0012.tlg001.perseus-grc2:15.497-15.499/)
- In Herodotus the queen offers Gyges herself and the kingship with τε … καί (1.11.2). Gyges gains the wife and the kingship with καί … καί (1.12.2). The same two referents appear with both patterns in a single narrative. These passages establish coexistence. The direction and date of reanalysis remain open. [The offer](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0016.tlg001.perseus-grc2:1.11.2/) · [The outcome](https://scaife.perseus.org/reader/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0016.tlg001.perseus-grc2:1.12.2/)

## Reproduce

From this directory, using Rocq 8.20.1:

```sh
coqc DoubleAdditive.v
coqchk -silent -o DoubleAdditive
```

The file uses only the standard library. It prints the assumptions of each of the five general results. CI compiles the same file and checks the kernel’s global-assumptions report. [Rocq source](DoubleAdditive.v)
